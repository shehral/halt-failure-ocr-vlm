## Framing note (P1): the deficit is a missing halt signal; "attractor" lives in the companion paper

P1's organizing claim is the **systems** one: the halt-failure deficit is a **missing halt
signal** (not a corrupted state, not mere repetition, not a single steerable layer), and
**single-site fixes do not install halting**. The on-disk evidence for the systems claim is
six converging perturbation nulls, all returning the pre-registered FAIL/null verdict (CL-12
B3 READOUT, verified on disk; CL-19/20 P16 FAIL N=5, verified on disk; CL-12b P10b FAIL N=2;
CL-12c P19 FAIL N=5; CL-12d P12 FAIL N=22; CL-12e P22 FAIL N=2 — these last four recorded
with FAIL verdicts, primary files pending re-sync), alongside the separate CL-49 L0
sufficiency null and the CL-36 norm-scaled positive control. Per CL-35, those nulls are
**equally consistent with (i) a genuinely distributed deficit and (ii) a perturbation
protocol under-powered at the halt-relevant locus**; the data do not distinguish the two.
**P1 does not need to resolve (i) vs (ii)** — the systems claim only requires that no
single-site intervention installs halting. The discriminating experiment (patching the same
readout direction at a non-loop position) is left to the **companion representational-geometry
paper** (repo `halt-failure-mechanism`) and to future work.

The "class-structured residual-stream attractor" reading, the per-class halt-direction
geometry, the two-family bifurcation, the count-manifold, the PCA/crosscoder fine-tune
signature, and build-vs-read are the **subject of the companion paper, not P1**. P1 mentions
they exist in a single "Mechanism (companion paper)" pointer and does not quote their effect
sizes. The P1 title/abstract/body/conclusion, and the README, all carry the missing-halt
framing consistently and route the representational "why" to the companion paper.
